Why Do I Abandon Projects at 95% Completion?

Last-Mile Completion Protocol is a check of the remaining acceptance, handoff and approval work needed to call a project complete.

Quick answer

Write the acceptance criteria and compare them with the actual deliverable.

What this guide is best for: readers who need to make a concrete decision about project finishing review.

How do you put this into practice?

Use the following sequence as a starting plan, then adjust it to the work you are responsible for.

  1. Write the acceptance criteria and compare them with the actual deliverable.
  2. Separate required fixes from optional polish; send the required work to its reviewer.
  3. Record the action you chose, its intended result and the next occasion on which you will check it. Keep the record in a place you can return to without rebuilding your setup.

How do you start project finishing review?

Write the acceptance criteria and compare them with the actual deliverable. Use a current commitment rather than a fictional ideal week. Check the actual calendar and inputs before treating the proposed action as feasible.

What should change your plan?

Separate required fixes from optional polish; send the required work to its reviewer. When the conditions change, record the new constraint and choose again. Tell anyone affected by a changed commitment rather than silently moving its deadline.

What mistake should you avoid?

Do not use a percentage complete as a substitute for a list of unmet conditions. If the method creates more work than it helps you resolve, simplify the record before adding another tool. The plan remains a proposal until you compare it with what actually happened.

What would this look like in practice?

A report has all its sections but lacks a checked source and recipient approval, so the next action is verification rather than another rewrite. This is an illustrative scenario, not a customer result or evidence of a measured improvement.

Frequently asked questions

What should you write down first?

Write the acceptance criteria and compare them with the actual deliverable. Keep the first record short enough to use during a real workday. Include the specific deliverable or decision and where the supporting information can be found.

How should you review the result?

Separate required fixes from optional polish; send the required work to its reviewer. Compare the result with the commitment you actually made. Identify one useful change for the next attempt rather than rewriting the entire system after a single difficult day.
