How the pillars work

The pillar system groups the site into durable operating domains rather than one-off tips. A pillar is a recurring area of execution that needs rules, language, and maintenance over time. This mirrors how the product itself treats planning: permanent categories, temporary projects, and explicit prioritization instead of equal-weight task lists.

Readers can use the pillars to move from a broad operating domain into a more specific page, then back into the product or answer pages when they want the commercial or system-level implementation. That makes the pillar index both navigational and explanatory.

Why the pillar model exists

The pillar model exists to organize the site around durable operating domains instead of random topics. That makes it easier to connect one-off problems back to the broader system and to keep the site architecture legible as it grows.

A strong operating system does not treat every page like a separate universe. It groups recurring concerns into stable categories, then links those categories back to the product and framework layers.
