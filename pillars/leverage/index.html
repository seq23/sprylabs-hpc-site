Leverage

What leverage means here

Leverage here means a move that creates disproportionate returns relative to the effort spent. It is not about looking busy. It is about identifying the action that changes the game, shortens future work, or compounds over time.

That can mean a decision memo, a reusable workflow, a single difficult email, a product page that converts, or a weekly review that prevents a month of drift. The common trait is that the action changes more than the hour it consumed.

This definition matters because people often confuse leverage with intensity. A system built on leverage gets calmer and stronger at the same time. A system built on brute force usually gets louder and more fragile.

Compounding advantage: delegation, automation, writing, distribution, and systems that scale impact.
