Continuity Over Intensity Rule — private journaling boundaries while recovering from burnout

Capacity is genuinely reduced, it has been for a while, and it is not going to bounce back tomorrow.

What is actually scarce here: capacity itself, which is the one input that cannot be reallocated from somewhere else.

Do this first. Declare a reduced-capacity mode in writing with a reduced standard, a list of suspended commitments, and a review date.

Do not do this. Do not run the normal standard against reduced capacity. It produces a run of failed days and a conclusion about yourself.

What should and should not go into a tool I do not control? Decide the categories in advance — work state and commitments in, personal health and third-party information out — and write the line down.

When this framework is the right one. Use it when planning a commitment you intend to keep for months, and the version you are about to commit to is the version you could do this week.

  1. Describe your worst plausible week honestly: illness, travel, a crisis at work.
  2. Size the commitment so it is still doable in that week, not in this one.
  3. Write the floor version explicitly, so that on a bad day there is something to do rather than a decision to make.
  4. Let good weeks overflow, but never let the overflow raise the floor.
