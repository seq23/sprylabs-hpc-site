Executive Review Mode — execution review while preparing for a high-stakes day

One day carries disproportionate consequence and the days before it are being consumed by anticipation.

What is actually scarce here: focus in the run-up, which anticipation takes without producing preparation.

Do this first. List what would actually change the outcome, cap the preparation at that list, and schedule the rest of the run-up as ordinary work.

Do not do this. Do not over-prepare. Past a point, preparation is anxiety management and it degrades the performance it is meant to support.

What actually happened over the last period, as distinct from how it felt? Read the written record rather than remembering, and draw exactly one conclusion per review.

When this framework is the right one. Use it when you already know most of what you need to know and the problem is that it is not organized into a decision.

  1. State the decision that is actually waiting, in one sentence ending in a verb.
  2. List only what you already know that bears on it. New research is out of scope for the session.
  3. Separate the list into facts, assumptions, and unknowns, and mark which unknowns would actually change the decision.
  4. Make the decision, or name the single piece of information that would settle it and how you will get it.
