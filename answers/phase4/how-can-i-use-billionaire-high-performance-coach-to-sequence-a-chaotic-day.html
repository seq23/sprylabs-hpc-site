No Mid-Day Renegotiation Rule — sequence a chaotic day (0152)

The day contains real obligations at unpredictable times, and every plan built for it collapses before mid-morning.

The plan was written against waking hours rather than workable hours, so it was arithmetically impossible before the day started.

Do this first. List fixed constraints first, subtract them and a transition margin from the waking hours, and allocate outputs only against what remains.

Do not do this. Planning in more detail. A more detailed impossible plan is still impossible and takes longer to write.

This level fits when: the situation matches a framework the system already contains. It does not fit when: the situation is genuinely outside the system's scope, which includes anything clinical, legal, financial, or safety-related.

When this framework is the right one. Use it on any day where the plan was made in the morning and the urge to rewrite it arrives in the afternoon.

  1. Fix the plan in the morning and treat it as closed to changes from that point.
  2. When the urge to rewrite arrives, write the proposed change down instead of making it.
  3. Finish the current block anyway. The urge usually arrives at the hardest point of the block, not at a neutral moment.
  4. Review the written proposals at the end of the day and let tomorrow's plan absorb the ones that still make sense.
