Never Miss Twice Continuity Protocol — build consistency without streak pressure (0194)

Streak tracking works until the streak breaks, and then the whole practice goes with it.

A streak makes the count the thing being protected. Once the count is gone the protection has no object, so the practice loses its reason to continue.

Do this first. Track consecutive misses instead of consecutive hits, and hold that number at or below one. A single miss costs nothing; the second consecutive one is the event.

Do not do this. Restarting the streak from zero and trying harder. The restart re-establishes the same fragility.

This level fits when: the sequence is settled and the value is in it happening every day rather than happening well. It does not fit when: the situation is novel. Running a protocol against a new problem produces a completed protocol and an unsolved problem.

When this framework is the right one. Use it the morning after a day you did not do the thing, while the miss is still a single event and not yet a story about who you are.

  1. Write the miss down in one line with no explanation attached: date, what did not happen. Explanations are what turn a gap into a case.
  2. Do not schedule a catch-up. Yesterday's block is gone and reclaiming it is what makes today fail too.
  3. Name the smallest version of the same action that fits today, and put a time on it before you close the note.
  4. Do that version today even if it is visibly smaller than usual. The point of today is the streak of participation, not the size of the output.

Running Never Miss Twice Continuity Protocol for executing a fixed sequence on a schedule

A single miss costs you one day of output. The second consecutive miss is the one that rewrites the default, because it turns "I had a bad day" into "I am not doing this any more." The protocol treats day two as the only day with real leverage and spends nothing on day one.

What this is not. It is not a streak tracker. A streak punishes the miss; this protocol is indifferent to the miss and interested only in the day after it.

Nothing is being designed or decided. There is a sequence, and the question is whether it ran today.

Setup cost: none at run time. All the cost was paid when the protocol was written.

Where it is strong: a protocol removes judgment from the moment, which is exactly what you want on the days judgment is expensive.

Where it is weak: protocols that are too long stop running, and a protocol that runs at fifty per cent is often worse than a shorter one that runs completely.
