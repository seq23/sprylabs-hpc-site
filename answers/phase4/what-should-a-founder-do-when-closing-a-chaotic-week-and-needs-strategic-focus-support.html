End-of-Day Evidence Loop — strategic focus while closing a chaotic week

The week is ending, it does not feel like anything happened, and the memory of it is dominated by two bad days.

What is actually scarce here: an accurate record. What is left is a reconstruction weighted toward the worst moments.

Do this first. List only what exists that did not exist on Monday, then read that list before writing anything about next week.

Do not do this. Do not plan next week from this feeling. A plan written against a bad reconstruction inherits the bad reconstruction.

How does the important work stop losing to the loud work? Name one foreground project for a period measured in weeks and give it every contested block by rule.

When this framework is the right one. Use it at the end of any day you plan to draw a conclusion from, especially days that felt bad.

  1. List what is finished, meaning something exists that did not exist this morning.
  2. List what is partial, and for each one write where it stopped so tomorrow starts at the edge rather than the beginning.
  3. List what was missed, without explanations. The explanation is tomorrow's problem, not tonight's.
  4. Write tomorrow's first output as a single sentence and stop.
