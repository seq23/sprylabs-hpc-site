Minimum Viable Day Recovery Protocol — accountability while stuck after a missed day

Yesterday did not happen and today is being spent deciding what to do about yesterday.

What is actually scarce here: nothing, actually. The capacity is there and it is being spent on the accounting.

Do this first. Log the miss in one line with no explanation, then run today's normal-size action at a fixed time.

Do not do this. Do not schedule a catch-up and do not write a post-mortem on a single miss.

How do I find out what I actually did rather than what I meant to do? Write checkable commitments in advance and score only artefacts that exist afterwards.

When this framework is the right one. Use it on a day where the honest read is that the normal plan is not going to happen, and the choice is between a smaller day and no day.

  1. Say the capacity out loud before you look at the list: what fraction of a normal day is actually available.
  2. Pick exactly one output that would make today non-zero, and define its finished state in a sentence.
  3. Delete or defer everything else in writing, so the deferral is a decision rather than a failure.
  4. Stop when the one output is done, even if energy returns. Stopping on purpose is what makes the smaller day repeatable.
