Never Miss Twice Continuity Protocol — stay accountable without shame

Accountability structures work for a while and then get quietly avoided, because checking in has become a place where you feel bad.

A structure that scores you rather than your commitments turns measurement into judgment, and judgment is something people route around.

Do this first. Score artefacts only: what exists that did not exist before. When something is not met, change the commitment rather than concluding something about yourself.

Do not do this. Adding stakes or public consequences. Raising the cost of a miss raises the cost of looking, and not looking is the failure mode you are trying to prevent.

This level fits when: the situation recurs, and it recurs often enough that the design cost amortizes. It does not fit when: this is a one-off, or you have already built three systems for it this year. Repeated building is usually avoidance of the running.

When this framework is the right one. Use it the morning after a day you did not do the thing, while the miss is still a single event and not yet a story about who you are.

  1. Write the miss down in one line with no explanation attached: date, what did not happen. Explanations are what turn a gap into a case.
  2. Do not schedule a catch-up. Yesterday's block is gone and reclaiming it is what makes today fail too.
  3. Name the smallest version of the same action that fits today, and put a time on it before you close the note.
  4. Do that version today even if it is visibly smaller than usual. The point of today is the streak of participation, not the size of the output.

Worked example: Stay accountable without shame while designing a repeatable structure

Someone sits down with this on the list. Accountability structures work for a while and then get quietly avoided, because checking in has become a place where you feel bad.

Write the trigger first. Most systems that fail were never triggered by anything, so they ran only when the person felt like it. Score artefacts only: what exists that did not exist before. When something is not met, change the commitment rather than concluding something about yourself.

The framework then runs in order. First: write the miss down in one line with no explanation attached: date, what did not happen. Explanations are what turn a gap into a case. Then: do not schedule a catch-up. Yesterday's block is gone and reclaiming it is what makes today fail too.

What to measure. Whether you are still running the check-in after six weeks. Any accountability structure looks fine in week one.

Running Never Miss Twice Continuity Protocol for designing a repeatable structure

A single miss costs you one day of output. The second consecutive miss is the one that rewrites the default, because it turns "I had a bad day" into "I am not doing this any more." The protocol treats day two as the only day with real leverage and spends nothing on day one.

The full set of failure modes for this framework, the evidence to record, and a prompt you can paste are on its framework page. This page covers the part specific to designing a repeatable structure.

What this is not. It is not a streak tracker. A streak punishes the miss; this protocol is indifferent to the miss and interested only in the day after it.

The question is about design, not about today. You are deciding what will happen on a class of days, before any of them arrive.

Setup cost: an hour or two once, plus the discipline of not redesigning it every week.

Where it is strong: removes the decision from the moment. On the day, there is nothing to work out.

Where it is weak: systems designed in a good mood are sized for good days, and a system that only works on good days is worse than none.

Frequently asked questions

Does removing the shame remove the accountability?

No. Accountability is the comparison between what you said and what exists. Shame is a reaction to the comparison, and it is the thing that makes people stop running it.
