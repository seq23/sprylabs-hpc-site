Execution Evidence Ledger — human executive coach for accountability

What human executive coach does well: brings judgment, accountability with a real relationship attached, and the ability to notice what you are not saying.

What it does not do: exist at 6am on a Tuesday when the decision is in front of you.

Do this first. The problem needs judgment, challenge, or a perspective genuinely outside your own.

Do not do this. Treating the two as substitutes. These are not substitutes. A structure that runs daily and a person who meets you fortnightly do different jobs, and the honest comparison is about which job is currently unmet.

How do I find out what I actually did rather than what I meant to do? Write checkable commitments in advance and score only artefacts that exist afterwards.

When this framework is the right one. Use it when you need to know what is actually happening over weeks rather than what it feels like is happening.

  1. Record one line per working day: the output that exists, in a form someone else could verify.
  2. Never record effort, intent, or how it felt. Those go elsewhere if you want them.
  3. Review weekly by reading the lines, not by remembering the week.
  4. Draw exactly one conclusion per review, and change one thing.

What to measure. Whether the thing you were missing is now happening, rather than whether you own a new tool.

Running Execution Evidence Ledger for accountability support

Memory of a period is reconstructed rather than recorded, and reconstruction is heavily weighted by the most recent and the most emotional days. A ledger written at the time is the only input to a review that is not itself a product of the review's mood.

The full set of failure modes for this framework, the evidence to record, and a prompt you can paste are on its framework page. This page covers the part specific to accountability support.

What this is not. It is not a time tracker and it does not measure hours. It measures artefacts.
