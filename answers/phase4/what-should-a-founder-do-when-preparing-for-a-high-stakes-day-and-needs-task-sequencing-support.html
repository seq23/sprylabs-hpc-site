Arbitration Engine — task sequencing while preparing for a high-stakes day

One day carries disproportionate consequence and the days before it are being consumed by anticipation.

What is actually scarce here: focus in the run-up, which anticipation takes without producing preparation.

Do this first. List what would actually change the outcome, cap the preparation at that list, and schedule the rest of the run-up as ordinary work.

Do not do this. Do not over-prepare. Past a point, preparation is anxiety management and it degrades the performance it is meant to support.

What order should these go in, given that some unblock others? Sequence by dependency first and use urgency only to break ties within a dependency tier.

When this framework is the right one. Use it when two or more things genuinely deserve the same block and the tie is not breaking on its own.

  1. Name the competing options explicitly, at most three. More than three is not arbitration, it is an unmade decision.
  2. Score each on leverage, urgency, energy fit, compounding, and downside if skipped.
  3. Say which dimension you are letting decide, before you look at the totals.
  4. Write the losing option's next review date so it is parked rather than abandoned.
