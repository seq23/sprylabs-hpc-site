First Hour Launch Sequence — build an operator rhythm (0188)

Work happens in bursts followed by gaps, and the gaps get longer, so output is unpredictable and hard to plan around.

Burst working is sized to a state rather than to a schedule. Because the state is not repeatable on demand, neither is the output.

Do this first. Fix the shape of the day at three points — the open, one protected block, and the close — and hold those three constant while everything else varies.

Do not do this. Scheduling the whole day. Full scheduling is fragile and breaks on the first real interruption, and once it breaks the rest of the day is improvised.

This level fits when: you have a design that has survived contact with at least one real day. It does not fit when: you are still redesigning. Installing an unsettled design means reinstalling repeatedly, which is how people conclude systems do not work for them.

When this framework is the right one. Use it on mornings where the first hour reliably disappears into inputs and the real work starts after lunch or not at all.

  1. Do one physical, non-screen action first. It exists to move you out of bed-state, not to be virtuous.
  2. Read one pre-written card that names today's first output. Deciding at 7am with no context is how the hour is lost.
  3. Work the first block on that output before opening any queue: mail, messages, feeds, tickets.
  4. Open the queues only after the block ends, and treat what is in them as input to the rest of the day rather than a re-plan.

What to measure. Whether the three fixed points happened, recorded daily as three binary fields. Rhythm is a frequency, so it can only be seen over weeks.
