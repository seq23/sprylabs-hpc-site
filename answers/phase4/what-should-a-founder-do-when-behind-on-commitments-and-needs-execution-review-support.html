No Catch-Up Closure Rule — execution review while behind on commitments

There is a set of things other people are waiting on, and the waiting is itself now part of the load.

What is actually scarce here: credibility with the people waiting, which decays faster from silence than from lateness.

Do this first. Send the status messages before doing any of the work. One line each: where it is, when it lands.

Do not do this. Do not disappear until you can deliver. Silence costs more than a late delivery with a date attached.

What actually happened over the last period, as distinct from how it felt? Read the written record rather than remembering, and draw exactly one conclusion per review.

When this framework is the right one. Use it when yesterday's undone list is about to be added on top of today's list.

  1. Put yesterday's undone items in a separate place from today's plan. Never merge the two lists.
  2. Give every carried item one of three dispositions: do today as a named block, reschedule to a named day, or drop.
  3. Write the dropped items down as dropped. A deletion you can see is closure; a deletion you cannot see comes back as pressure.
  4. Build today's plan only after the carried list is empty of undecided items.

Worked example: a founder with three unowned items and one investor update due

It is 09:40. The investor update is due Friday, two hires are waiting on a decision that only they can make, and a customer email arrived that will take an hour to answer properly. Nothing on that list has an owner other than them. There is a set of things other people are waiting on, and the waiting is itself now part of the load.

Running No Catch-Up Closure Rule for execution review support

Undone work does not carry a deadline forward with it; it carries guilt forward. A carried list makes today structurally impossible, which produces another carried list. The rule forces an explicit disposition for every carried item so that today's list is a list someone could actually finish.
