Accountability Mirror Protocol — stay accountable without shame

Accountability structures work for a while and then get quietly avoided, because checking in has become a place where you feel bad.

A structure that scores you rather than your commitments turns measurement into judgment, and judgment is something people route around.

Do this first. Score artefacts only: what exists that did not exist before. When something is not met, change the commitment rather than concluding something about yourself.

Do not do this. Adding stakes or public consequences. Raising the cost of a miss raises the cost of looking, and not looking is the failure mode you are trying to prevent.

This level fits when: you need one answer now and you are not going to do this repeatedly. It does not fit when: you have asked the same kind of question more than three times. At that point the retyping is the cost, and it is worth installing something.

When this framework is the right one. Use it when your sense of how the week went and what actually got finished have started to disagree.

  1. Write the commitment before the period starts, in a form that can be checked without interpretation.
  2. At the end of the period, list only what exists: files, sent messages, shipped changes, decisions recorded.
  3. Put the two lists side by side and mark each commitment met, partially met, or not met.
  4. Change one thing for the next period, and change the commitment rather than the effort.

Running Accountability Mirror Protocol for using a general assistant chat

Self-assessment drifts because it is scored on effort and intent, both of which are visible from the inside. The mirror scores only on artefacts, which are visible from the outside, and shows the gap between what was committed and what exists.

The full set of failure modes for this framework, the evidence to record, and a prompt you can paste are on its framework page. This page covers the part specific to using a general assistant chat.

What this is not. It is not a performance review and it is not for anyone else to read. It is a measurement instrument, and instruments that shame you get switched off.

You are working inside an ordinary chat window with no saved setup. Whatever structure exists has to be typed into the message you are about to send.

Setup cost: none. This is the mode you are already in, which is why it is the one people actually use.

Where it is strong: zero friction to start, and it works on any device, any account, any day.

Where it is weak: nothing persists. The quality of the answer is set entirely by what you happened to type this time, so the same question gets a different answer on a different day.
