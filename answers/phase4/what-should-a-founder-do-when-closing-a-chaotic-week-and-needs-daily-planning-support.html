Accountability Mirror Protocol — daily planning while closing a chaotic week

The week is ending, it does not feel like anything happened, and the memory of it is dominated by two bad days.

What is actually scarce here: an accurate record. What is left is a reconstruction weighted toward the worst moments.

Do this first. List only what exists that did not exist on Monday, then read that list before writing anything about next week.

Do not do this. Do not plan next week from this feeling. A plan written against a bad reconstruction inherits the bad reconstruction.

How does a list become a plan that fits the day I actually have? Subtract fixed constraints and a transition margin from waking hours, then allocate only the remainder.

When this framework is the right one. Use it when your sense of how the week went and what actually got finished have started to disagree.

  1. Write the commitment before the period starts, in a form that can be checked without interpretation.
  2. At the end of the period, list only what exists: files, sent messages, shipped changes, decisions recorded.
  3. Put the two lists side by side and mark each commitment met, partially met, or not met.
  4. Change one thing for the next period, and change the commitment rather than the effort.
