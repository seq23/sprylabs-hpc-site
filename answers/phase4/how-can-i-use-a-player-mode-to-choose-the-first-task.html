Choosing at the start of a block means choosing at the moment you have the least context and the most inertia. The decision is being made in the worst possible conditions.

Do this first. Write the first output the night before, as one sentence, somewhere you will physically see it in the morning. Do not decide in the morning.

Do not do this. Ranking the whole list. Ranking is a much larger task than choosing one item, and it is frequently how the block gets spent.

This level fits when: you want the same standard applied across sessions and you are willing to re-seed it. It does not fit when: the task genuinely needs a different standard. Forcing one mode onto everything is how modes stop meaning anything.

When this framework is the right one. Use it when two or more things genuinely deserve the same block and the tie is not breaking on its own.

  1. Name the competing options explicitly, at most three. More than three is not arbitration, it is an unmade decision.
  2. Score each on leverage, urgency, energy fit, compounding, and downside if skipped.
  3. Say which dimension you are letting decide, before you look at the totals.
  4. Write the losing option's next review date so it is parked rather than abandoned.
