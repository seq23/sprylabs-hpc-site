Arbitration Engine — separate urgency from pressure (0177)

Everything feels urgent, and the things that get done are the loud ones rather than the consequential ones.

Urgency and internal pressure produce the same sensation, so an internally generated deadline competes on equal terms with an external one and usually wins because it is closer.

Do this first. For each item, ask what actually breaks if it waits a week, and who outside your head would notice. Real urgency has an external answer.

This level fits when: you have a design that has survived contact with at least one real day. It does not fit when: you are still redesigning. Installing an unsettled design means reinstalling repeatedly, which is how people conclude systems do not work for them.

When this framework is the right one. Use it when two or more things genuinely deserve the same block and the tie is not breaking on its own.

  1. Name the competing options explicitly, at most three. More than three is not arbitration, it is an unmade decision.
  2. Score each on leverage, urgency, energy fit, compounding, and downside if skipped.
  3. Say which dimension you are letting decide, before you look at the totals.
  4. Write the losing option's next review date so it is parked rather than abandoned.
