Continuity Over Intensity Rule — task sequencing while stuck after a missed day

Yesterday did not happen and today is being spent deciding what to do about yesterday.

What is actually scarce here: nothing, actually. The capacity is there and it is being spent on the accounting.

Do this first. Log the miss in one line with no explanation, then run today's normal-size action at a fixed time.

Do not do this. Do not schedule a catch-up and do not write a post-mortem on a single miss.

What order should these go in, given that some unblock others? Sequence by dependency first and use urgency only to break ties within a dependency tier.

When this framework is the right one. Use it when planning a commitment you intend to keep for months, and the version you are about to commit to is the version you could do this week.

  1. Describe your worst plausible week honestly: illness, travel, a crisis at work.
  2. Size the commitment so it is still doable in that week, not in this one.
  3. Write the floor version explicitly, so that on a bad day there is something to do rather than a decision to make.
  4. Let good weeks overflow, but never let the overflow raise the floor.
