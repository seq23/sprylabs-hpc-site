Arbitration Engine — accountability while overwhelmed

Every item on the list feels equally urgent, which is the reliable sign that none of them is being assessed.

What is actually scarce here: attention, not time. The hours may exist; what is missing is the ability to hold one thing still long enough to work it.

Do this first. Take one item off the list, out loud, and work it for twenty-five minutes without looking at the rest.

Do not do this. Do not re-plan. Re-planning under overwhelm produces a longer list and a stronger sense of how much there is.

How do I find out what I actually did rather than what I meant to do? Write checkable commitments in advance and score only artefacts that exist afterwards.

When this framework is the right one. Use it when two or more things genuinely deserve the same block and the tie is not breaking on its own.

  1. Name the competing options explicitly, at most three. More than three is not arbitration, it is an unmade decision.
  2. Score each on leverage, urgency, energy fit, compounding, and downside if skipped.
  3. Say which dimension you are letting decide, before you look at the totals.
  4. Write the losing option's next review date so it is parked rather than abandoned.

Worked example: a founder with three unowned items and one investor update due

It is 09:40. The investor update is due Friday, two hires are waiting on a decision that only they can make, and a customer email arrived that will take an hour to answer properly. Nothing on that list has an owner other than them.

Every item on the list feels equally urgent, which is the reliable sign that none of them is being assessed.

The accountability move is specific: write checkable commitments in advance and score only artefacts that exist afterwards. The list is the same size and one item is smaller. That is the only reliable move from this state.

What to measure. Met, partial, and not-met counts, plus the one change made for the next period.

Running Arbitration Engine for accountability support

Ties do not break by thinking harder, because the two options are close on the dimension you are thinking about. Scoring them on five named dimensions moves the comparison somewhere the difference is visible, and forces the trade to be stated rather than felt.
