Billionaire Mindset Track — make a hard decision

The decision has been open for weeks. Each time you return to it you rehearse the same considerations and stop in the same place.

Rehearsal is not analysis. The options are close on the dimension you keep thinking about, so thinking about that dimension harder cannot separate them.

Do this first. Score the options on leverage, urgency, energy fit, compounding, and downside if skipped, and name which dimension you are letting decide before you total anything.

Do not do this. Gathering more information. Most of what you would gather does not change the answer, and gathering is a comfortable way to keep the decision open.

This level fits when: the behavior you want is stable and you want it by default. It does not fit when: you are still working out what you want. Configuring an unsettled preference makes it harder to change.

When this framework is the right one. Use it when an opportunity is in front of you and the question is whether it is worth entering at all.

  1. Ask what you would own at the end, not what you would be paid during.
  2. Ask whether the work is repeatable without you, or whether it stops the day you stop.
  3. Ask whether the payoff distribution is asymmetric: is there a version where this is large.
  4. Ask what the worst realistic outcome costs, and whether you can carry that cost without it ending other things.

Worked example: Make a hard decision while configuring standing assistance

Someone sits down with this on the list. The decision has been open for weeks. Each time you return to it you rehearse the same considerations and stop in the same place.

Write the standing instruction, then open a completely fresh context and check it behaves the same way. Score the options on leverage, urgency, energy fit, compounding, and downside if skipped, and name which dimension you are letting decide before you total anything.

The framework then runs in order. First: ask what you would own at the end, not what you would be paid during. Then: ask whether the work is repeatable without you, or whether it stops the day you stop.

What to measure. Whether the decision was made and written down with its deciding dimension. An unwritten decision reopens.

Running Billionaire Mindset Track for configuring standing assistance

Opportunities are usually evaluated on upside, which is the dimension the person presenting it has already optimized. Scoring ownership, leverage, asymmetry, and downside containment moves the evaluation to dimensions that determine whether the upside would ever reach you.

The full set of failure modes for this framework, the evidence to record, and a prompt you can paste are on its framework page. This page covers the part specific to configuring standing assistance.

What this is not. It is not financial advice, investment guidance, or a claim about how anyone actually became wealthy. It is four questions applied in a fixed order.

You are putting something in place that will be there tomorrow without being rebuilt: standing instructions, a stored template, a routine you can invoke by name.

Setup cost: a short configuration session, plus a cold test in a fresh context.

Where it is strong: it applies without you remembering to apply it, which is where most of the value is.

Where it is weak: configuration is invisible once set, so a bad rule keeps operating silently until you go and read it again.
