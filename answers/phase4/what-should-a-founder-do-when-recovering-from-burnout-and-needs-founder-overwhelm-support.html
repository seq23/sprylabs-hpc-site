Minimum Viable Day Recovery Protocol — founder overwhelm while recovering from burnout

Capacity is genuinely reduced, it has been for a while, and it is not going to bounce back tomorrow.

What is actually scarce here: capacity itself, which is the one input that cannot be reallocated from somewhere else.

Do this first. Declare a reduced-capacity mode in writing with a reduced standard, a list of suspended commitments, and a review date.

Do not do this. Do not run the normal standard against reduced capacity. It produces a run of failed days and a conclusion about yourself.

Everything is mine and everything is urgent — where does the first cut go? Separate items with an external consequence from items with an internal one, and order by the external answer.

When this framework is the right one. Use it on a day where the honest read is that the normal plan is not going to happen, and the choice is between a smaller day and no day.

  1. Say the capacity out loud before you look at the list: what fraction of a normal day is actually available.
  2. Pick exactly one output that would make today non-zero, and define its finished state in a sentence.
  3. Delete or defer everything else in writing, so the deferral is a decision rather than a failure.
  4. Stop when the one output is done, even if energy returns. Stopping on purpose is what makes the smaller day repeatable.
