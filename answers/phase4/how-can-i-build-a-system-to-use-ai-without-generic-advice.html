This level fits when: the situation recurs, and it recurs often enough that the design cost amortizes. It does not fit when: this is a one-off, or you have already built three systems for it this year. Repeated building is usually avoidance of the running.

When this framework is the right one. Use it when you already know most of what you need to know and the problem is that it is not organized into a decision.

  1. State the decision that is actually waiting, in one sentence ending in a verb.
  2. List only what you already know that bears on it. New research is out of scope for the session.
  3. Separate the list into facts, assumptions, and unknowns, and mark which unknowns would actually change the decision.
  4. Make the decision, or name the single piece of information that would settle it and how you will get it.

Running Executive Review Mode for designing a repeatable structure

When a situation is unclear, the reflex is to gather more input, and more input makes an already-unstructured picture harder to act on. Review mode forbids new material and forces the existing material into a shape a decision can be made from.

The full set of failure modes for this framework, the evidence to record, and a prompt you can paste are on its framework page. This page covers the part specific to designing a repeatable structure.

What this is not. It is not strategy generation and it will not tell you something you did not already know. It converts what you know into a decision.

The question is about design, not about today. You are deciding what will happen on a class of days, before any of them arrive.

Setup cost: an hour or two once, plus the discipline of not redesigning it every week.

Where it is strong: removes the decision from the moment. On the day, there is nothing to work out.

Where it is weak: systems designed in a good mood are sized for good days, and a system that only works on good days is worse than none.
