Recovery Mode Operating Rule — build consistency without streak pressure (0154)

Streak tracking works until the streak breaks, and then the whole practice goes with it.

A streak makes the count the thing being protected. Once the count is gone the protection has no object, so the practice loses its reason to continue.

Do this first. Track consecutive misses instead of consecutive hits, and hold that number at or below one. A single miss costs nothing; the second consecutive one is the event.

Do not do this. Restarting the streak from zero and trying harder. The restart re-establishes the same fragility.

This level fits when: the situation matches a framework the system already contains. It does not fit when: the situation is genuinely outside the system's scope, which includes anything clinical, legal, financial, or safety-related.

When this framework is the right one. Use it in a stretch of days where capacity is genuinely reduced and is not going to bounce back tomorrow.

  1. Declare the mode explicitly and give it an end date or a review date. An undeclared reduction is just a decline.
  2. Set the reduced standard in writing: what a completed day looks like while the mode is on.
  3. Suspend anything that only works at full capacity, by name, rather than letting it fail quietly.
  4. Review on the date. Either exit the mode or extend it deliberately, and if you extend twice, get outside input.
