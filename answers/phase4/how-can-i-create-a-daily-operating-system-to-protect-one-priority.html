Foreground Priority Selection Rule — protect one priority (0096)

Several projects are technically active, all of them get some attention, and none of them has produced a visible result in weeks.

Parallel projects share time in whatever proportion the day's noise dictates, which usually means no project gets a block long enough to reach a result.

Do this first. Name one project foreground for a period measured in weeks, give every other active project a written maintenance floor, and let the foreground take every contested block.

Do not do this. Trying to give each project a fair share. Fairness at the project level produces failure at every project.

This level fits when: your days are structurally similar enough that one loop can cover them. It does not fit when: your days differ wildly in shape. Then the loop belongs at the week level with a small daily floor.

When this framework is the right one. Use it when several projects are all technically active and none of them is visibly moving.

  1. Name one project as foreground for a stated period, measured in weeks rather than days.
  2. Give every other active project an explicit background status with a maintenance floor: the minimum that keeps it from decaying.
  3. When a block is contested, the foreground project takes it. This is the entire rule and its whole value is that it is decided in advance.
  4. Review the foreground choice on a fixed date, not when a background project starts feeling urgent.

Running Foreground Priority Selection Rule for building a day-level operating loop

Parallel projects do not share time evenly; they share it in whatever proportion the day's noise dictates, which usually means none of them gets a block long enough to produce a result. Naming one foreground project does not stop the others; it decides in advance which one wins a contested block.

The full set of failure modes for this framework, the evidence to record, and a prompt you can paste are on its framework page. This page covers the part specific to building a day-level operating loop.

What this is not. It is not a ban on other work. Background projects continue at their maintenance floor; they just stop winning contested blocks.

The unit is the day. The question is what happens at the open, what happens during, and what happens at the close, every day, in the same order.

Setup cost: a design session plus roughly two weeks of running it before judging it.

Where it is strong: because it is the same every day, the parts that are wrong become visible fast.

Where it is weak: day-level systems are the easiest to over-design, and every additional step multiplies across every day.
