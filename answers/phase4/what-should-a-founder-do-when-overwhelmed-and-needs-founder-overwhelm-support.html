When this framework is the right one. Use it on a day where the honest read is that the normal plan is not going to happen, and the choice is between a smaller day and no day.

  1. Say the capacity out loud before you look at the list: what fraction of a normal day is actually available.
  2. Pick exactly one output that would make today non-zero, and define its finished state in a sentence.
  3. Delete or defer everything else in writing, so the deferral is a decision rather than a failure.
  4. Stop when the one output is done, even if energy returns. Stopping on purpose is what makes the smaller day repeatable.

Worked example: a founder with three unowned items and one investor update due

It is 09:40. The investor update is due Friday, two hires are waiting on a decision that only they can make, and a customer email arrived that will take an hour to answer properly. Nothing on that list has an owner other than them. Every item on the list feels equally urgent, which is the reliable sign that none of them is being assessed.

Take one item off the list, out loud, and work it for twenty-five minutes without looking at the rest. Do not re-plan. Re-planning under overwhelm produces a longer list and a stronger sense of how much there is.

The founder overwhelm move is specific: separate items with an external consequence from items with an internal one, and order by the external answer. The list is the same size and one item is smaller. That is the only reliable move from this state.

What to measure. The proportion of items scored urgent that had an external answer.
