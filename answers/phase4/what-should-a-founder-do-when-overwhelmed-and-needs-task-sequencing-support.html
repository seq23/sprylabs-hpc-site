Capacity-Matched Execution Rule — task sequencing while overwhelmed

Every item on the list feels equally urgent, which is the reliable sign that none of them is being assessed.

What is actually scarce here: attention, not time. The hours may exist; what is missing is the ability to hold one thing still long enough to work it.

Do this first. Take one item off the list, out loud, and work it for twenty-five minutes without looking at the rest.

Do not do this. Do not re-plan. Re-planning under overwhelm produces a longer list and a stronger sense of how much there is.

What order should these go in, given that some unblock others? Sequence by dependency first and use urgency only to break ties within a dependency tier.

When this framework is the right one. Use it when the block in front of you does not match the state you are actually in, and the choice looks like force it or lose it.

  1. Read your current state honestly and quickly: high, medium, or low. Do not negotiate with it.
  2. Categorize your work the same way, in advance, so the matching does not require a decision when you are already low.
  3. Take work from the matching category. A low block spent on low-category work is a productive block.
  4. Note mismatches. Repeated mismatch at the same hour is a scheduling fact, not a discipline fact.
