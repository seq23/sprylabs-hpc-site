Execution Evidence Ledger — behavioral follow-through while behind on commitments

There is a set of things other people are waiting on, and the waiting is itself now part of the load.

What is actually scarce here: credibility with the people waiting, which decays faster from silence than from lateness.

Do this first. Send the status messages before doing any of the work. One line each: where it is, when it lands.

Do not do this. Do not disappear until you can deliver. Silence costs more than a late delivery with a date attached.

Why does the thing that was decided not get done? Ask whether it was visible, scoped, scheduled, and possible at that capacity, and change the first answer that is no.

When this framework is the right one. Use it when you need to know what is actually happening over weeks rather than what it feels like is happening.

  1. Record one line per working day: the output that exists, in a form someone else could verify.
  2. Never record effort, intent, or how it felt. Those go elsewhere if you want them.
  3. Review weekly by reading the lines, not by remembering the week.
  4. Draw exactly one conclusion per review, and change one thing.

Worked example: a founder with three unowned items and one investor update due

It is 09:40. The investor update is due Friday, two hires are waiting on a decision that only they can make, and a customer email arrived that will take an hour to answer properly. Nothing on that list has an owner other than them. There is a set of things other people are waiting on, and the waiting is itself now part of the load.

Send the status messages before doing any of the work. One line each: where it is, when it lands. Do not disappear until you can deliver. Silence costs more than a late delivery with a date attached.

The behavioral follow-through move is specific: ask whether it was visible, scoped, scheduled, and possible at that capacity, and change the first answer that is no. The load is unchanged and the pressure drops sharply, because most of it was the silence rather than the work.
