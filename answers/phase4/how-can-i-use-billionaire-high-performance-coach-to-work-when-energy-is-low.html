Continuity Over Intensity Rule — work when energy is low (0155)

A block arrives, the state does not match the work, and the hour goes to rereading the same paragraph.

Work has an energy profile and so does a block. Mismatch produces a stalled hour, and the stall gets attributed to discipline rather than to the mismatch.

Do this first. Categorize your work as high, medium, or low in advance, then take work from the category that matches the state you are actually in.

Do not do this. Forcing high-category work through a low block. It produces an unproductive hour and a conclusion about yourself, and neither is useful.

This level fits when: the situation matches a framework the system already contains. It does not fit when: the situation is genuinely outside the system's scope, which includes anything clinical, legal, financial, or safety-related.

When this framework is the right one. Use it when planning a commitment you intend to keep for months, and the version you are about to commit to is the version you could do this week.

  1. Describe your worst plausible week honestly: illness, travel, a crisis at work.
  2. Size the commitment so it is still doable in that week, not in this one.
  3. Write the floor version explicitly, so that on a bad day there is something to do rather than a decision to make.
  4. Let good weeks overflow, but never let the overflow raise the floor.

Running Continuity Over Intensity Rule for running the packaged system as installed

Effort is elastic and schedules are not. A commitment sized to a good week fails in a normal one, and each failure costs more than the extra output of the good week bought. Sizing to the worst plausible week produces a commitment that survives, and surviving is what accumulates.
