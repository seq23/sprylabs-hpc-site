Never Miss Twice Continuity Protocol — motivation dependency while returning after drift

The system stopped some time ago, the exact date is unclear, and returning feels like it requires an explanation.

What is actually scarce here: a starting point. Everything is equally stopped so there is no obvious first move.

Do this first. Restart one component today, chosen because the others depend on it, and leave everything else stopped for a week.

Do not do this. Do not analyze the drift before restarting. The analysis becomes the project and the system stays stopped.

What happens on the days the motivation is not there? Size the commitment against the worst plausible week and write the floor version explicitly.

When this framework is the right one. Use it the morning after a day you did not do the thing, while the miss is still a single event and not yet a story about who you are.

  1. Write the miss down in one line with no explanation attached: date, what did not happen. Explanations are what turn a gap into a case.
  2. Do not schedule a catch-up. Yesterday's block is gone and reclaiming it is what makes today fail too.
  3. Name the smallest version of the same action that fits today, and put a time on it before you close the note.
  4. Do that version today even if it is visibly smaller than usual. The point of today is the streak of participation, not the size of the output.

Running Never Miss Twice Continuity Protocol for motivation dependency support

A single miss costs you one day of output. The second consecutive miss is the one that rewrites the default, because it turns "I had a bad day" into "I am not doing this any more." The protocol treats day two as the only day with real leverage and spends nothing on day one.

What this is not. It is not a streak tracker. A streak punishes the miss; this protocol is indifferent to the miss and interested only in the day after it.
