What is actually scarce here: focus in the run-up, which anticipation takes without producing preparation.

Do this first. List what would actually change the outcome, cap the preparation at that list, and schedule the rest of the run-up as ordinary work.

Do not do this. Do not over-prepare. Past a point, preparation is anxiety management and it degrades the performance it is meant to support.

Everything is mine and everything is urgent — where does the first cut go? Separate items with an external consequence from items with an internal one, and order by the external answer.

When this framework is the right one. Use it at the start of a day where you know roughly what matters but the list has no order and no fit to the hours available.

  1. List fixed constraints first: meetings, travel, care obligations, anything already committed. These are not negotiable inputs.
  2. Subtract them from the waking hours to get the real number of workable hours, then subtract a further margin for transition and overrun.
  3. Fill the remaining hours with named outputs in priority order, stopping when the hours run out rather than when the list runs out.
  4. Everything below the cut goes to the deferred list, in writing, before the day starts.
