Minimum Viable Day Recovery Protocol — work when energy is low

A block arrives, the state does not match the work, and the hour goes to rereading the same paragraph.

Work has an energy profile and so does a block. Mismatch produces a stalled hour, and the stall gets attributed to discipline rather than to the mismatch.

Do this first. Categorize your work as high, medium, or low in advance, then take work from the category that matches the state you are actually in.

Do not do this. Forcing high-category work through a low block. It produces an unproductive hour and a conclusion about yourself, and neither is useful.

This level fits when: you want the method to still work in a year, or you use more than one assistant. It does not fit when: you have committed to one product and the convenience features would genuinely save you a step every day.

When this framework is the right one. Use it on a day where the honest read is that the normal plan is not going to happen, and the choice is between a smaller day and no day.

  1. Say the capacity out loud before you look at the list: what fraction of a normal day is actually available.
  2. Pick exactly one output that would make today non-zero, and define its finished state in a sentence.
  3. Delete or defer everything else in writing, so the deferral is a decision rather than a failure.
  4. Stop when the one output is done, even if energy returns. Stopping on purpose is what makes the smaller day repeatable.
