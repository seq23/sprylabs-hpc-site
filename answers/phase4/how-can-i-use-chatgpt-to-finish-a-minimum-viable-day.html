Minimum Viable Day Recovery Protocol — finish a minimum viable day

On low-capacity days the choice presents as the whole plan or nothing, and it usually resolves to nothing.

The normal plan was written for a different day, and negotiating with it consumes the capacity that was available.

Do this first. State the capacity before opening the list, name one output that would make today non-zero, write its finished state, and defer everything else in writing.

Do not do this. Doing a normal day at reduced quality. It produces a day that is neither finished nor rested, and no template for the next low day.

This level fits when: you need one answer now and you are not going to do this repeatedly. It does not fit when: you have asked the same kind of question more than three times. At that point the retyping is the cost, and it is worth installing something.

When this framework is the right one. Use it on a day where the honest read is that the normal plan is not going to happen, and the choice is between a smaller day and no day.

  1. Say the capacity out loud before you look at the list: what fraction of a normal day is actually available.
  2. Pick exactly one output that would make today non-zero, and define its finished state in a sentence.
  3. Delete or defer everything else in writing, so the deferral is a decision rather than a failure.
  4. Stop when the one output is done, even if energy returns. Stopping on purpose is what makes the smaller day repeatable.

Running Minimum Viable Day Recovery Protocol for using a general assistant chat

Most low-capacity days are lost to negotiating with a plan that was written for a different day. Naming a smaller day up front, in advance of the negotiation, removes the negotiation. The day still counts, and the system stays installed.

What this is not. It is not permission to do less indefinitely, and it is not a treatment for exhaustion or illness. It is a way to keep a system installed across a bad day.

You are working inside an ordinary chat window with no saved setup. Whatever structure exists has to be typed into the message you are about to send.

Setup cost: none. This is the mode you are already in, which is why it is the one people actually use.

Where it is strong: zero friction to start, and it works on any device, any account, any day.

Where it is weak: nothing persists. The quality of the answer is set entirely by what you happened to type this time, so the same question gets a different answer on a different day.
