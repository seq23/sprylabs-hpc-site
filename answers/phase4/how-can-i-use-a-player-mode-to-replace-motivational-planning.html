Capacity-Matched Execution Rule — replace motivational planning (0150)

Plans get made in a motivated state and executed in an ordinary one, and the ordinary state cannot carry them.

A plan written while motivated is sized for the motivation. Motivation is not a resource you can schedule, so the plan is effectively sized for conditions that will not reliably recur.

Do this first. Describe your worst plausible week and size the commitment so it still works in that week. Write the floor version explicitly so bad days have an action rather than a decision.

Do not do this. Trying to sustain the motivated state. It is not sustainable, and building on it means rebuilding after every dip.

This level fits when: you want the same standard applied across sessions and you are willing to re-seed it. It does not fit when: the task genuinely needs a different standard. Forcing one mode onto everything is how modes stop meaning anything.

When this framework is the right one. Use it when the block in front of you does not match the state you are actually in, and the choice looks like force it or lose it.

  1. Read your current state honestly and quickly: high, medium, or low. Do not negotiate with it.
  2. Categorize your work the same way, in advance, so the matching does not require a decision when you are already low.
  3. Take work from the matching category. A low block spent on low-category work is a productive block.
  4. Note mismatches. Repeated mismatch at the same hour is a scheduling fact, not a discipline fact.
