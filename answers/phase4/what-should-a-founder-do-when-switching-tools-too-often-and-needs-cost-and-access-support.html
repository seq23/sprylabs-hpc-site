System Drift Recovery Protocol — cost and access while switching tools too often

Setup has happened more recently than output, and there is a new tool in the last month.

What is actually scarce here: accumulated state. Every switch discards the history that made the previous tool useful.

Do this first. Pick the tool you have the most history in, commit to it for a stated period, and write down what would have to be true to switch.

Do not do this. Do not evaluate another tool. Evaluation is the activity that is substituting for the work.

What does this actually require, and what happens if I cannot pay for the convenient version? Separate what the method needs from what a particular product offers, and keep the method in a form that works without the product.

When this framework is the right one. Use it when a system you were running has quietly stopped and you are considering a full rebuild.

  1. List the components of the system as it was, without judging them.
  2. Mark each one running, intermittent, or stopped, using evidence rather than memory.
  3. Restart exactly one stopped component, the one the others depend on most.
  4. Wait a week before restarting a second. Drift usually came from running more components than the week supports.

Running System Drift Recovery Protocol for cost and access support

Drift is almost never total: most of the system is intact and one or two components stopped. A rebuild discards the intact parts along with the broken ones and costs a week, which is why the rebuild usually drifts too. Recovery finds the specific stopped component and restarts only that.
