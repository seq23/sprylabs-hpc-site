Executive Review Mode — make a hard decision (0114)

The decision has been open for weeks. Each time you return to it you rehearse the same considerations and stop in the same place.

Rehearsal is not analysis. The options are close on the dimension you keep thinking about, so thinking about that dimension harder cannot separate them.

Do this first. Score the options on leverage, urgency, energy fit, compounding, and downside if skipped, and name which dimension you are letting decide before you total anything.

Do not do this. Gathering more information. Most of what you would gather does not change the answer, and gathering is a comfortable way to keep the decision open.

This level fits when: answers keep coming back plausible and unusable, which is the signature of an unbounded request. It does not fit when: you are exploring and do not yet know what good looks like. Constraints applied to exploration close it early.

When this framework is the right one. Use it when you already know most of what you need to know and the problem is that it is not organized into a decision.

  1. State the decision that is actually waiting, in one sentence ending in a verb.
  2. List only what you already know that bears on it. New research is out of scope for the session.
  3. Separate the list into facts, assumptions, and unknowns, and mark which unknowns would actually change the decision.
  4. Make the decision, or name the single piece of information that would settle it and how you will get it.

Running Executive Review Mode for converting an open-ended tool into a bounded one

When a situation is unclear, the reflex is to gather more input, and more input makes an already-unstructured picture harder to act on. Review mode forbids new material and forces the existing material into a shape a decision can be made from.

The full set of failure modes for this framework, the evidence to record, and a prompt you can paste are on its framework page. This page covers the part specific to converting an open-ended tool into a bounded one.

What this is not. It is not strategy generation and it will not tell you something you did not already know. It converts what you know into a decision.

The problem is not capability, it is that the tool will do anything, and anything is a bad default when you are trying to decide one thing.

Setup cost: the work of writing down constraints you have been holding implicitly.

Where it is strong: a bounded tool gives comparable answers across days, which is the precondition for noticing that something changed.

Where it is weak: constraints written too tightly produce answers that are technically compliant and useless.
