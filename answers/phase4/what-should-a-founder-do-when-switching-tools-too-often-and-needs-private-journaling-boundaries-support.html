What is actually scarce here: accumulated state. Every switch discards the history that made the previous tool useful.

Do this first. Pick the tool you have the most history in, commit to it for a stated period, and write down what would have to be true to switch.

Do not do this. Do not evaluate another tool. Evaluation is the activity that is substituting for the work.

What should and should not go into a tool I do not control? Decide the categories in advance — work state and commitments in, personal health and third-party information out — and write the line down.

When this framework is the right one. Use it when the explanation for a repeated failure has become a statement about your character.

  1. State the failure as an event with a time and a place, not as a trait.
  2. Ask the four structural questions: was it visible, was it scoped, was it scheduled, was it possible at that capacity.
  3. Change the first structural answer that is no, and change only that one.
  4. Observe for a stated period before concluding anything about yourself.
