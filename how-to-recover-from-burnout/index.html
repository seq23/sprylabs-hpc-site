Workload review during exhaustion: practical guide

Workload review during exhaustion is an organizational review of commitments and capacity, not a diagnosis or treatment for burnout. Identify obligations that need immediate coverage and tasks that can be deferred with communication.

What this guide is best for: readers who need to make a concrete decision about workload review during exhaustion.

How do you put this into practice?

Use the following sequence as a starting plan, then adjust it to the work you are responsible for.

  1. Identify obligations that need immediate coverage and tasks that can be deferred with communication.
  2. If exhaustion is persistent or affects daily functioning, seek appropriate professional support while reviewing workload.
  3. Record the action you chose, its intended result and the next occasion on which you will check it. Keep the record in a place you can return to without rebuilding your setup.

How can you communicate a reduced workload without making a health claim?

Describe the operational constraint and the decision needed from the other person. You might explain that you cannot complete two agreed deliverables by the same deadline and ask which should take priority. Name the work that can still be delivered, the work that needs a new date and any information needed to make the choice. You do not need to diagnose yourself in order to raise a capacity problem.

Keep a written record of the revised agreement so expectations do not depend on memory. Treat a planning adjustment as a way to manage commitments, not as proof of recovery or a substitute for care. If symptoms persist, worsen or affect daily functioning, seek support from a qualified health professional. Return to the plan after the workload decision and check whether the revised demands are feasible under the conditions you actually face.

What would this look like in practice?

An employee tells their manager that current capacity cannot cover two deadlines and asks for a clear priority and coverage plan. This is an illustrative scenario, not a customer result or evidence of a measured improvement.
