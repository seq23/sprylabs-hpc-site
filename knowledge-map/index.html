Spry Executive OS practical planning guides

Spry Executive OS is a collection of practical planning guides published by Spry Labs. The guides explain how to choose work, maintain a routine, review progress and use AI for structured reflection. Each topic has one primary guide, with examples, boundaries and a route to the publisher’s system manual.

Quick answer

Executive OS: principles, rules and routines

What this guide is best for: readers choosing the guide that matches the work or planning problem in front of them.

How do you put this into practice?

Use the following sequence as a starting plan, then adjust it to the work you are responsible for.

  • Executive OS: principles, rules and routines — operators who have several planning tools but no agreed way to choose and finish the next task.
  • Reset cycle: restart a routine after a missed day — people who repeatedly replace a useful routine because an imperfect week feels like a failed plan.
  • Phone vortex meaning: interrupt the checking loop — people whose phone checks interrupt work even when the phone also has legitimate uses during the day.
  • Personal scorecard: choose measures you can act on — operators who collect plenty of metrics but cannot explain how those measures change the next week’s plan.
  • Deep work: plan a focus block you can finish — people who reserve time for focused work but spend much of that time deciding where to begin.
  • AI coaching at scale: define privacy and oversight — team leads evaluating a bounded planning pilot rather than promising private, autonomous coaching to an entire workforce.
  • Executive coaching prompts: context to commitment — leaders who want a reusable session structure instead of a long list of disconnected prompt tricks.
  • Structure ChatGPT conversations for ongoing work — people who revisit ongoing work in chat and find that the original constraints or decisions have become unclear.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need an AI tool to use an executive OS?

No. A notebook, calendar and a short review record can carry the method. An AI assistant may help turn your notes into a proposed plan, but you still choose the priority, verify the facts and decide what may be shared. Start with the simplest place you will actually revisit.

How do I know the system needs changing?

Change a rule when the review shows a recurring mismatch between the plan and the work you are responsible for. Record an example before revising it. If interruptions repeatedly displace the priority, change the start condition or capacity assumption rather than rebuilding the entire planning system after one difficult day.
