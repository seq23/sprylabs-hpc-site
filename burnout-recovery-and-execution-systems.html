The 3 Stages of Structured Recovery

Stage 1: Stabilize Capacity

Objective: reduce load and stop further depletion. Entry condition: normal work repeatedly causes shutdown or prolonged recovery. Operating limit: essentials and one small completion loop.

Promotion condition: basic obligations can be completed without a next-day crash.

Stage 2: Rebuild a Minimum Execution Floor

Objective: restore reliable participation at low intensity. Entry condition: capacity is more stable but still inconsistent. Operating limit: one foreground action plus maintenance floors.

Promotion condition: the minimum cadence holds across several uneven days.

Stage 3: Expand Without Recreating the Old Load

Objective: increase output while keeping recovery protected. Entry condition: the minimum cadence is stable. Operating limit: add one demand at a time and preserve stop rules.

Promotion condition: higher output remains sustainable without returning to chronic overload.

This framework is educational and organizational. It does not diagnose or treat burnout or replace medical or mental-health care.

burnout recovery and execution systems: direct answer

Direct answer: Burnout recovery and execution systems should protect capacity before pushing output. In Spry Executive OS, the recovery move is to reduce the execution floor, remove catch-up pressure, preserve continuity, and return to normal intensity only after the system is stable again.

Definition: Recovery Execution System

A recovery execution system protects continuity while reducing intensity. It avoids catch-up pressure and uses minimum viable actions to rebuild capacity.
