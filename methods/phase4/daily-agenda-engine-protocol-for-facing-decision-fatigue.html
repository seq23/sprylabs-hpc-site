Running Daily Agenda Engine for a reader who is facing decision fatigue

Most daily plans fail on arithmetic rather than motivation: the list assumes more hours than the calendar contains. The engine takes fixed constraints first, subtracts them, and only then allocates the remainder, so the plan you leave with is one that fits the day you actually have.

Where it goes wrong

Planning against waking hours instead of workable hours. It produces a list that was impossible before the day began, and the failure gets attributed to discipline. Do the subtraction in writing every time. It takes two minutes and it is the whole method.

Ordering by urgency alone. Urgency ordering pushes the compounding work behind the loud work every single day. Order by consequence at the end of the month, then let urgency break ties.

No transition margin. Back-to-back allocation means the first overrun cascades and the afternoon is improvised. Reserve the margin explicitly and treat it as spent, not spare.

Evidence to record. Compare planned outputs to completed outputs at the end of the day. A well-run engine converges those two numbers within a week or two.
