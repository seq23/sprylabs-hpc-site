Running Never Miss Twice Continuity Protocol for a reader who is closing a chaotic week

A single miss costs you one day of output. The second consecutive miss is the one that rewrites the default, because it turns "I had a bad day" into "I am not doing this any more." The protocol treats day two as the only day with real leverage and spends nothing on day one.

Where it goes wrong

Doubling up to make the miss disappear. A doubled block is harder than a normal one, so the day after a miss becomes the hardest day of the week. That is the wrong incentive to attach to returning. Return at normal size or smaller. Never at larger.

Writing a post-mortem on day one. Analysis of a single miss produces a theory about yourself, and theories about yourself are much harder to miss twice against. Log the fact, skip the cause. Look for causes only after a third miss, where a pattern actually exists.

Restarting the count. Treating a miss as a reset to zero makes the count the thing you are protecting rather than the work. Track consecutive misses, not consecutive hits. The number you care about is never allowed above one.

Evidence to record. At the end of the week, count how many times two misses landed back to back. That number, not the total days completed, tells you whether the protocol is working.

A line you can paste. I missed yesterday. Do not analyze it. Give me the smallest version of the same action that fits today, and a time to start it.

What this is not. It is not a streak tracker. A streak punishes the miss; this protocol is indifferent to the miss and interested only in the day after it.
