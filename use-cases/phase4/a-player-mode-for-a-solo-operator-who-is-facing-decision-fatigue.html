Decision Fatigue Reduction Protocol — a solo operator who is facing decision fatigue

You are the whole operation: delivery, admin, sales, and the switching between them.

The hardest part of the week: protecting a block long enough to finish delivery work before an admin item interrupts it.

Do this first. List the decisions you made yesterday that you also made the day before. Those are the recurring ones.

Do not do this. Writing defaults without override conditions. A default with no override becomes a rule you break, and a rule you break undermines the others.

Small decisions are taking disproportionately long and the big ones are being deferred. Do not try to decide better. The problem is the count, not the quality.

When this framework is the right one. Use it when the decisions that feel hardest are small ones, which is the usual sign that the count is the problem rather than the difficulty.

  1. List the decisions you made yesterday that you also made the day before. Those are the recurring ones.
  2. For each, write a default: what happens unless something specific overrides it.
  3. Write the override condition too, so the default is a rule rather than a rut.
  4. Re-decide only when the override condition fires, and review the defaults on a fixed schedule rather than continuously.

What is hardest about this for a solo operator?

Protecting a block long enough to finish delivery work before an admin item interrupts it. Context switching is your largest hidden cost and it does not appear on any list.

What should a solo operator not do while facing decision fatigue?

Do not try to decide better. The problem is the count, not the quality. Every default gets an override condition written at the same time.

What should a reader do in the first week on solo operator?

What should a reader do in the first week on facing decision fatigue?

Tally the decisions you make twice a day. Convert none of them yet. Tiredness. It responds to removing choices rather than to rest, which is how you tell them apart.
