Execution Evidence Ledger — a executive who is closing a chaotic week

Most of your calendar is committed by other people and the residue is where your own work has to fit.

The hardest part of the week: doing anything that requires more than twenty-five uninterrupted minutes.

Do this first. Record one line per working day: the output that exists, in a form someone else could verify.

Do not do this. Backfilling a week at a time. A backfilled week is a reconstruction, which is what the ledger exists to replace.

The week is ending, it does not feel like anything happened, and the memory of it is dominated by two bad days. Do not plan next week from this feeling. A plan written against a bad reconstruction inherits the bad reconstruction.

When this framework is the right one. Use it when you need to know what is actually happening over weeks rather than what it feels like is happening.

  1. Record one line per working day: the output that exists, in a form someone else could verify.
  2. Never record effort, intent, or how it felt. Those go elsewhere if you want them.
  3. Review weekly by reading the lines, not by remembering the week.
  4. Draw exactly one conclusion per review, and change one thing.
