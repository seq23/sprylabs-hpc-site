Accountability Mirror Protocol — a consultant who is behind on commitments

Client work is billable and visible; your own business development is neither.

The hardest part of the week: doing pipeline work during a busy delivery month, which is when it matters most.

Do this first. Write the commitment before the period starts, in a form that can be checked without interpretation.

Do not do this. Scoring effort. Effort is always available as an explanation, so any period can be scored as a success and the mirror stops reflecting anything.

There is a set of things other people are waiting on, and the waiting is itself now part of the load. Do not disappear until you can deliver. Silence costs more than a late delivery with a date attached.

When this framework is the right one. Use it when your sense of how the week went and what actually got finished have started to disagree.

  1. Write the commitment before the period starts, in a form that can be checked without interpretation.
  2. At the end of the period, list only what exists: files, sent messages, shipped changes, decisions recorded.
  3. Put the two lists side by side and mark each commitment met, partially met, or not met.
  4. Change one thing for the next period, and change the commitment rather than the effort.

Frequently asked questions

What is hardest about this for a consultant?

Doing pipeline work during a busy delivery month, which is when it matters most. Non-billable hours, which are the first thing sacrificed when a client escalates.

What should a consultant not do while behind on commitments?

Do not disappear until you can deliver. Silence costs more than a late delivery with a date attached. Score artefacts only. Effort goes in a separate note if you want it recorded.

What should a reader do in the first week on consultant?

Put one recurring non-billable block in the calendar and treat it as booked. A sales problem. It is usually a scheduling problem: pipeline work has no protected slot.

What should a reader do in the first week on behind on commitments?

Send the status messages before doing any of the work, every day, until the outstanding list is visible to the people waiting. A capacity problem. It is usually a communication problem first and a capacity problem second.
