Ambiguity Stop Rule — a high performer who is overplanning instead of executing

The current standard is being sustained by effort, and effort is a resource with a limit.

The hardest part of the week: reducing anything, because everything is currently working.

Do this first. Stop trying to start. The re-reading is the diagnosis, not a discipline failure.

Do not do this. Pushing through with willpower. It sometimes works and the cost is that you learn to spend your scarcest resource on a formatting problem.

The plan has been refined more than twice and no block of work has started. Do not improve the planning process. A better planning process is a more comfortable place to stay.

When this framework is the right one. Use it the moment you notice you have read the same list item three times without starting it.

  1. Stop trying to start. The re-reading is the diagnosis, not a discipline failure.
  2. Ask what the first physical thing your hands would do. If there is no answer, the item is not a task.
  3. If a decision is hidden inside it, extract the decision and make that the item.
  4. Rewrite the item as the physical action, and leave the original phrasing next to it so the pattern is visible later.
