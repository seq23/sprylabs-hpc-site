First Hour Launch Sequence — a high performer who is needing a lower-friction plan

The current standard is being sustained by effort, and effort is a resource with a limit.

The hardest part of the week: reducing anything, because everything is currently working.

Do this first. Do one physical, non-screen action first. It exists to move you out of bed-state, not to be virtuous.

Do not do this. A quick check of messages first. There is no such thing; the queue sets the mode on contact, and the block afterwards is spent thinking about the queue.

The plan is correct and it is not being followed, and the reason each time is a small piece of friction. Do not add discipline. Friction is a design property and discipline is an expensive way to pay for it daily.

When this framework is the right one. Use it on mornings where the first hour reliably disappears into inputs and the real work starts after lunch or not at all.

  1. Do one physical, non-screen action first. It exists to move you out of bed-state, not to be virtuous.
  2. Read one pre-written card that names today's first output. Deciding at 7am with no context is how the hour is lost.
  3. Work the first block on that output before opening any queue: mail, messages, feeds, tickets.
  4. Open the queues only after the block ends, and treat what is in them as input to the rest of the day rather than a re-plan.
