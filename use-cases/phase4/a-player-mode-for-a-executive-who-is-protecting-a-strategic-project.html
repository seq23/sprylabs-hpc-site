No Mid-Day Renegotiation Rule — a executive who is protecting a strategic project

Most of your calendar is committed by other people and the residue is where your own work has to fit.

The hardest part of the week: doing anything that requires more than twenty-five uninterrupted minutes.

Do this first. Fix the plan in the morning and treat it as closed to changes from that point.

Do not do this. Treating every mood as an external change. It makes the rule unenforceable, because there is always a justification available.

There is one project that matters more than the rest and it keeps losing blocks to things that are louder. Do not rely on intention to protect it. Intention loses to urgency every time, in the moment, reliably.

When this framework is the right one. Use it on any day where the plan was made in the morning and the urge to rewrite it arrives in the afternoon.

  1. Fix the plan in the morning and treat it as closed to changes from that point.
  2. When the urge to rewrite arrives, write the proposed change down instead of making it.
  3. Finish the current block anyway. The urge usually arrives at the hardest point of the block, not at a neutral moment.
  4. Review the written proposals at the end of the day and let tomorrow's plan absorb the ones that still make sense.
