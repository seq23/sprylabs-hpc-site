Continuity Over Intensity Rule — a creative who is trying to build consistency

Output quality varies with state more than with hours, and the state is not reliably schedulable.

The hardest part of the week: producing on a schedule without the schedule flattening the work.

Do this first. Describe your worst plausible week honestly: illness, travel, a crisis at work.

Do not do this. Raising the floor after a good stretch. It converts a sustainable commitment into one sized for conditions that will not persist, and the next bad week breaks it.

The practice works for a stretch and then stops, and the stops are not caused by anything in particular. Do not raise the target. The target is not the problem; the absence of a floor is.

When this framework is the right one. Use it when planning a commitment you intend to keep for months, and the version you are about to commit to is the version you could do this week.

  1. Describe your worst plausible week honestly: illness, travel, a crisis at work.
  2. Size the commitment so it is still doable in that week, not in this one.
  3. Write the floor version explicitly, so that on a bad day there is something to do rather than a decision to make.
  4. Let good weeks overflow, but never let the overflow raise the floor.

Frequently asked questions

What is hardest about this for a creative?

Producing on a schedule without the schedule flattening the work. Usable creative state, which arrives unpredictably and is spent quickly.

What should a creative not do while trying to build consistency?

Do not raise the target. The target is not the problem; the absence of a floor is. Raise the floor only after several bad weeks cleared it easily.

What should a reader do in the first week on creative?

Separate generative work from finishing work in the schedule and stop asking one block to do both. A discipline problem. State-dependent output is a scheduling problem, not a character one.

What should a reader do in the first week on trying to build consistency?

Define the floor and run only the floor, including on the days you could do more. Needing more willpower. What is missing is a floor: a version small enough that bad days have an action rather than a decision.
