Arbitration Engine — a career switcher who is scattered across priorities

The work that matters is unpaid and competes with work that is paid and immediate.

The hardest part of the week: keeping the switch alive across weeks where the current job takes everything.

Do this first. Name the competing options explicitly, at most three. More than three is not arbitration, it is an unmade decision.

Do not do this. Scoring more than three options. Beyond three the grid becomes a research project and the block is spent on the grid.

Several projects are active, each has had some attention this week, and none has produced anything visible. Do not try to give each project a fair share. Fairness at the project level produces failure at every project.

When this framework is the right one. Use it when two or more things genuinely deserve the same block and the tie is not breaking on its own.

  1. Name the competing options explicitly, at most three. More than three is not arbitration, it is an unmade decision.
  2. Score each on leverage, urgency, energy fit, compounding, and downside if skipped.
  3. Say which dimension you are letting decide, before you look at the totals.
  4. Write the losing option's next review date so it is parked rather than abandoned.

Frequently asked questions

What is hardest about this for a career switcher?

Keeping the switch alive across weeks where the current job takes everything. Evening and weekend hours, which are also the hours when capacity is lowest.

What should a career switcher not do while scattered across priorities?

Do not try to give each project a fair share. Fairness at the project level produces failure at every project. Cut to three by inspection first. The excluded items usually exclude themselves.

What should a reader do in the first week on career switcher?

Fix two named evenings and defend them the way you would defend a paid commitment. Needing more commitment. The hours are genuinely contested and the commitment is usually not the missing part.

What should a reader do in the first week on scattered across priorities?

Name one foreground project and give it the first block daily. Leave the others exactly as they are. Being busy. Busy produces output; scattered produces motion across several things and output in none.
