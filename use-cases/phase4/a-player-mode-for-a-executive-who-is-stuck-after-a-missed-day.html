When this framework is the right one. Use it the morning after a day you did not do the thing, while the miss is still a single event and not yet a story about who you are.

  2. Do not schedule a catch-up. Yesterday's block is gone and reclaiming it is what makes today fail too.
  3. Name the smallest version of the same action that fits today, and put a time on it before you close the note.
  4. Do that version today even if it is visibly smaller than usual. The point of today is the streak of participation, not the size of the output.

Frequently asked questions

What is hardest about this for a executive?

Doing anything that requires more than twenty-five uninterrupted minutes. Contiguous time. The gaps between meetings are numerous and none of them is long enough.

What should a executive not do while stuck after a missed day?

Do not schedule a catch-up and do not write a post-mortem on a single miss. Return at normal size or smaller. Never at larger.

What should a reader do in the first week on executive?

Match work to gap length in advance so a thirty-minute window never gets a task that needs ninety. Needing more time. The calendar is not going to yield; the residue has to be used differently.

What should a reader do in the first week on stuck after a missed day?

Log misses in one line with no explanation and run the normal-size action the next day. Nothing else. Losing momentum. One missed day removes almost no momentum; the day spent accounting for it removes a lot.
