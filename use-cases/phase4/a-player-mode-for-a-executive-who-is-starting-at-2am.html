Capacity-Matched Execution Rule — a executive who is starting at 2am

Most of your calendar is committed by other people and the residue is where your own work has to fit.

The hardest part of the week: doing anything that requires more than twenty-five uninterrupted minutes.

Do this first. Read your current state honestly and quickly: high, medium, or low. Do not negotiate with it.

Do not do this. Categorizing in the moment. Deciding a category while low produces the category that justifies not working.

The work is happening at hours that do not repeat, driven by whatever state made tonight possible. Do not build a system around the good night. It is sized for a state you cannot schedule.

When this framework is the right one. Use it when the block in front of you does not match the state you are actually in, and the choice looks like force it or lose it.

  1. Read your current state honestly and quickly: high, medium, or low. Do not negotiate with it.
  2. Categorize your work the same way, in advance, so the matching does not require a decision when you are already low.
  3. Take work from the matching category. A low block spent on low-category work is a productive block.
  4. Note mismatches. Repeated mismatch at the same hour is a scheduling fact, not a discipline fact.
