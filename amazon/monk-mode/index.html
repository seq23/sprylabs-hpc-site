The protocol, on one page

Step 1: Declare one output, not a theme

A block aimed at "getting in shape" or "working on the business" has no finish line, so it cannot be evaluated. Name the artifact that will exist at the end: the manuscript, the shipped feature, the completed application. If you cannot name the artifact, you are not ready to start the block.

Step 2: Write the suspension list before day one

Monk Mode is defined by what you stop, not by what you add. List the specific commitments, projects and inputs that are paused, and the date they resume. An unwritten suspension list quietly becomes a list of things you feel guilty about instead of a list of things you decided.

Step 3: Fix the end date and let it be real

The block ends on its date whether the output is finished or not. A block that extends whenever the work is unfinished is not a block; it is an open-ended sprint, and it produces the burnout the method exists to avoid.

Step 4: Set the daily minimum low enough to survive a bad day

The minimum is the amount you will do on your worst day of the block, not your best. Most abandoned blocks are abandoned two days after a minimum was set at a level that only a good day supports.

Step 5: Run the exit review on the last day

Write down what the block produced, what the suspension list actually cost, and which suspended item you did not miss. That last answer is usually the most valuable output of the block, and it is the one people skip.

Where this usually goes wrong

Treating intensity as the variable

Monk Mode is a subtraction method. If the block feels like the same life plus more effort, the suspension list was never written or was never honoured, and the block will end when energy runs out rather than on its date.

Stacking a second block immediately

The review is part of the method. Running a new block the day after one ends discards the information the first block produced and compounds the fatigue it accumulated.

Using a block to avoid a decision

A block is for executing a decision already made. If the output is unclear because the underlying choice is unresolved, the block will be spent relitigating the choice.

Announcing the block instead of running it

Telling people you are entering Monk Mode creates the social reward that finishing the output was supposed to provide. Announce the suspension to the people it affects; skip the audience.
