The exit ramp, on one page

Step 1: Name the exact moment action stops

Paralysis has a location. Write the sentence that describes it — "I stop when I open the document and see the blank brief" — rather than "I procrastinate." The moment tells you which of the remaining steps you actually need.

Step 2: Reduce the visible options to one chosen output

Every additional visible option is another decision being made while you are already stuck. Pick one criterion, choose with it, and park the rest somewhere written down so parking them does not feel like losing them.

Step 3: Choose a reversible first action that produces information

The first action's job is not progress; it is to make the task less unknown. Reversible means a bad choice costs nothing, which is what lets you make it while uncertain.

Step 4: Set a short stop point

Starting has to stop implying endless work. A defined stop point — twenty minutes, one section, one page — is what makes beginning cheap enough to actually do.

Step 5: Leave a restart note before the block ends

One line about where you stopped and what comes next. Without it, the next session begins with the same blank-page moment you just escaped, and the ramp has to be run again from step one.

Where this usually goes wrong

Calling yourself lazy

It is a verdict, not a location, and nothing follows from it. "I cannot start" and "I will not start" need different responses, and only one of them has anything to do with motivation.

Expanding the plan while overwhelmed

Planning feels like progress and adds decisions. When stuck, cut to the single next physical action; decide whether more planning is needed only after that action is done.

Applying urgency to a fear-shaped block

Urgency works on tasks that are merely unpleasant. When the block is fear, exhaustion, illness or grief, urgency raises the cost of approaching the task and deepens the avoidance.

Skipping the restart note because the session went well

The note is cheapest to write while the context is still in your head and most valuable on the day you cannot reconstruct it. Good sessions are exactly when it gets skipped.
