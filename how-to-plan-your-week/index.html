Weekly commitment plan: practical guide

Weekly commitment plan is a review of deadlines, capacity and dependencies across the next working week. Collect commitments from your calendar and messages and identify missing inputs.

What this guide is best for: readers who need to make a concrete decision about weekly commitment plan.

How do you put this into practice?

Use the following sequence as a starting plan, then adjust it to the work you are responsible for.

  1. Collect commitments from your calendar and messages and identify missing inputs.
  2. Contact dependency owners before scheduling work that cannot start without their input.
  3. Record the action you chose, its intended result and the next occasion on which you will check it. Keep the record in a place you can return to without rebuilding your setup.

How can you plan a week when several people depend on you?

Begin with promises rather than a blank list of preferred activities. For each deliverable, identify who is waiting for it, what they need to receive and what information you still need from someone else. Put requests for those inputs early enough that a delayed reply can be noticed. Separate work you can complete independently from work that depends on approval or a shared meeting.

Reserve a review point before the final deadline so you can communicate a risk while alternatives still exist. Do not treat every open hour as available production time; include the recurring responsibilities you already know you must handle. At the weekly review, examine a commitment that moved. Decide whether the cause was missing information, an unrealistic estimate or a deliberate change in priority.

Use that distinction to adjust the following week rather than copying an unfinished list forward unchanged.
