AI-supported reflection: practical guide

AI-supported reflection is the use of an assistant to organize a self-reported work problem and suggest questions for human review.

Quick answer

Describe one decision or missed commitment without sharing sensitive details.

What this guide is best for: readers who need to make a concrete decision about ai-supported reflection.

How do you put this into practice?

Use the following sequence as a starting plan, then adjust it to the work you are responsible for.

  1. Describe one decision or missed commitment without sharing sensitive details.
  2. Use the output as a draft of questions and verify any factual or professional claims separately.
  3. Record the action you chose, its intended result and the next occasion on which you will check it. Keep the record in a place you can return to without rebuilding your setup.

How do you start ai-supported reflection?

Describe one decision or missed commitment without sharing sensitive details. Use a current commitment rather than a fictional ideal week. Check the actual calendar and inputs before treating the proposed action as feasible.

What should change your plan?

Use the output as a draft of questions and verify any factual or professional claims separately. When the conditions change, record the new constraint and choose again. Tell anyone affected by a changed commitment rather than silently moving its deadline.

What mistake should you avoid?

Do not describe an assistant as a licensed professional or a substitute for clinical support. If the method creates more work than it helps you resolve, simplify the record before adding another tool. The plan remains a proposal until you compare it with what actually happened.

Frequently asked questions

What should you write down first?

Describe one decision or missed commitment without sharing sensitive details. Keep the first record short enough to use during a real workday. Include the specific deliverable or decision and where the supporting information can be found.

How should you review the result?

Use the output as a draft of questions and verify any factual or professional claims separately. Compare the result with the commitment you actually made. Identify one useful change for the next attempt rather than rewriting the entire system after a single difficult day.
