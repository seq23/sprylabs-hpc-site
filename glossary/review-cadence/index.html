Review cadence means the fixed schedule on which you look back at your work and decide what changes: a short daily close, a weekly review of priorities and commitments, and a monthly or quarterly review of goals. A good cadence is set in advance and kept regardless of mood, so drift is caught within days rather than months.

What is a good review cadence?

Daily: 5-10 minutes to close the day and set tomorrow's first action. Weekly: 30-45 minutes to compare commitments with what happened and choose next week's one priority. Quarterly: 60-90 minutes to check goals against results and drop what no longer matters.

Why does review cadence matter more than the review template?

Because a review that happens on schedule catches drift while it is still cheap to fix. Most systems fail not because the template is wrong but because reviews are skipped after a bad week, which is exactly when they are needed.

How do you keep a review cadence after missing one?

Do the next scheduled review, not a catch-up. Skip the backlog, review only the current week and apply never-miss-twice: one missed review is noise, two in a row is the signal to shorten the review rather than abandon it.

Frequently asked questions

What does review cadence mean?

Review cadence is how often, and on what fixed schedule, you review your work and plans - for example a daily close, a weekly review and a quarterly reset. The cadence is the rhythm; the review is what you do inside it.

What is a review cadence?

It is the agreed frequency of reviews for a plan, a project or a personal system. Setting it in advance turns reviewing from something you do when you remember into an appointment you keep.

What does cadence review mean?

A cadence review checks the cadence itself: whether your daily, weekly and quarterly reviews are actually happening and are the right length. Review cadence is the schedule; a cadence review audits it.
