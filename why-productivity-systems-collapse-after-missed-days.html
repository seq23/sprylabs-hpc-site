Productivity systems collapse after missed days when they treat interruption like failure. That turns one break in continuity into a full identity reset.

Why people struggle

Consistency collapses when a system only has one gear. The moment energy drops, the whole plan feels invalid, so people miss a day, interpret it as failure, and restart from scratch.

What actually works

The stronger pattern is a continuity architecture with fallback modes. A minimum viable day, a clear re-entry rule, and a tiny proof-of-work standard keep the system alive even when conditions are bad.
