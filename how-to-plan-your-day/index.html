Daily capacity plan: practical guide

Daily capacity plan is a schedule that fits chosen work around fixed commitments and explicitly leaves room for uncertainty. Put fixed appointments and necessary preparation on the calendar before choosing optional work.

What this guide is best for: readers who need to make a concrete decision about daily capacity plan.

How do you put this into practice?

Use the following sequence as a starting plan, then adjust it to the work you are responsible for.

  1. Put fixed appointments and necessary preparation on the calendar before choosing optional work.
  2. Use the remaining capacity to choose a deliverable rather than copying an entire backlog into today.
  3. Record the action you chose, its intended result and the next occasion on which you will check it. Keep the record in a place you can return to without rebuilding your setup.

What should you do when a new request arrives during the day?

First establish what the request needs and when the result is actually required. A notification is an arrival time, not necessarily a deadline. Compare the request with the commitments already on your calendar. If accepting it would displace something promised to another person, identify that tradeoff before agreeing.

Write down which task would move, who needs to know, and what can still be completed today. If the request can wait, place it in the next review rather than keeping it open as a second active task. If it must happen now, leave a short restart note in the interrupted work: the file you were using, the question you were answering and the next physical action. Close the day by checking whether the changed plan was communicated and whether any unfinished commitment needs an explicit new agreement.
