Executive coaching prompts: context to commitment

Executive coaching prompts are instructions for a structured reflection with an AI assistant about a work goal, decision or leadership situation. They provide relevant context, ask for questions before recommendations and end with an explicit next action. The assistant supports preparation and reflection; it does not become a qualified human coach or make decisions for you.

Quick answer

State the decision or situation in neutral terms, removing information you are not authorized to share. Include the constraint that makes the issue difficult.

What this guide is best for: leaders who want a reusable session structure instead of a long list of disconnected prompt tricks.

How do you put this into practice?

Use the following sequence as a starting plan, then adjust it to the work you are responsible for.

  1. State the decision or situation in neutral terms, removing information you are not authorized to share. Include the constraint that makes the issue difficult.
  2. Ask the assistant to separate supplied facts, assumptions and unanswered questions before proposing a plan. Correct its interpretation before continuing.
  3. Request one clarifying question at a time. Answer only what is relevant to the decision, and decline questions that would require confidential details.
  4. Ask for feasible options and the tradeoffs for each. Verify claims about people, policy or external facts using an appropriate source.
  5. Choose the next action yourself and record its owner and review condition outside the chat. An agreeable answer is not evidence that the action happened.

What is a useful prompt for a difficult decision?

Try: Help me examine this decision using only the facts I supply. First identify my objective, constraints and uncertainties. Ask a clarifying question before suggesting options. For each option, name a tradeoff and an assumption I should verify. Finish by asking which next action I choose. This is an example structure, not a guarantee of reliable advice.

How can a manager prepare a conversation?

Ask for open questions that would help you understand the situation rather than a diagnosis of the other person. A useful instruction is: Help me prepare a conversation about this missed commitment; separate my observations from interpretations and suggest questions about barriers and expectations. Review the suggestions yourself, and keep the actual conversation under human responsibility.

How should you finish an AI reflection session?

End with a decision record that distinguishes what you know from what remains uncertain. State the next action, the person responsible and when or under what condition it will be reviewed. Save it in the system you actually use for commitments. Without that handoff, a well-written chat can become another place where plans are stored but never revisited.

What would this look like in practice?

A founder is unsure whether to accept a new request while a delivery is late. The prompt asks for constraints and missing information before advice. The assistant’s proposed plan assumes a teammate is available; the founder corrects that assumption. The chosen action is to confirm delivery capacity before replying. The chat helps reveal a question, while the founder retains authority over the commitment.

Frequently asked questions

Can a prompt turn ChatGPT into an executive coach?

A prompt can shape a conversation, but it does not supply professional qualifications, personal accountability or verified knowledge about your situation. Treat the output as material to review. A human coach or other qualified adviser may be appropriate when the issue requires experience, a continuing relationship or responsibilities a software assistant cannot provide.

Should you paste private meeting notes into a prompt?

Only use information that your approved environment and organizational policies permit. Remove unnecessary personal, customer and transaction details before preparing the prompt. Even a useful summary can expose confidential context. If you cannot determine whether the environment is approved for the material, use a nonconfidential description or keep the preparation outside the tool.

What makes a prompt reusable?

A reusable prompt defines the session’s purpose, required context, questions, boundaries and ending. Keep those elements stable while replacing the situation-specific facts. After using it, check whether the session produced a clear action or revealed a missing fact. Revise the structure when it repeatedly produces generic advice or unsupported assumptions.
