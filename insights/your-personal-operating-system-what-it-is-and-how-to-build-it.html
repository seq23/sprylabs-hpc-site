Personal operating system: practical guide

Personal operating system is a personal agreement about how you capture obligations, choose work and review commitments. List the places where promises currently live, including messages, notebooks and calendars.

Quick answer

List the places where promises currently live, including messages, notebooks and calendars.

What this guide is best for: readers who need to make a concrete decision about personal operating system.

How do you put this into practice?

Use the following sequence as a starting plan, then adjust it to the work you are responsible for.

  1. List the places where promises currently live, including messages, notebooks and calendars.
  2. Keep one commitments list and use your calendar only for work that needs a time.
  3. Record the action you chose, its intended result and the next occasion on which you will check it. Keep the record in a place you can return to without rebuilding your setup.

What should change your plan?

Keep one commitments list and use your calendar only for work that needs a time. When the conditions change, record the new constraint and choose again. Tell anyone affected by a changed commitment rather than silently moving its deadline.

What mistake should you avoid?

Do not migrate every old note before testing the next real commitment. If the method creates more work than it helps you resolve, simplify the record before adding another tool. The plan remains a proposal until you compare it with what actually happened.
