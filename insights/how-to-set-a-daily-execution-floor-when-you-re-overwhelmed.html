Minimum daily action: practical guide

Minimum daily action is the smallest action that preserves a current commitment’s purpose on a constrained day. Identify the obligation that matters today and define a useful action small enough to start.

Quick answer

Identify the obligation that matters today and define a useful action small enough to start.

What this guide is best for: readers who need to make a concrete decision about minimum daily action.

How do you put this into practice?

Use the following sequence as a starting plan, then adjust it to the work you are responsible for.

  1. Identify the obligation that matters today and define a useful action small enough to start.
  2. Keep critical obligations visible and communicate when even the minimum cannot be completed.
  3. Record the action you chose, its intended result and the next occasion on which you will check it. Keep the record in a place you can return to without rebuilding your setup.

How do you start minimum daily action?

Identify the obligation that matters today and define a useful action small enough to start. Use a current commitment rather than a fictional ideal week. Check the actual calendar and inputs before treating the proposed action as feasible.

What should change your plan?

Keep critical obligations visible and communicate when even the minimum cannot be completed. When the conditions change, record the new constraint and choose again. Tell anyone affected by a changed commitment rather than silently moving its deadline.

What mistake should you avoid?

Do not call a symbolic action sufficient when someone else depends on a substantive deliverable. If the method creates more work than it helps you resolve, simplify the record before adding another tool. The plan remains a proposal until you compare it with what actually happened.

Frequently asked questions

What should you write down first?

Identify the obligation that matters today and define a useful action small enough to start. Keep the first record short enough to use during a real workday. Include the specific deliverable or decision and where the supporting information can be found.

How should you review the result?

Keep critical obligations visible and communicate when even the minimum cannot be completed. Compare the result with the commitment you actually made. Identify one useful change for the next attempt rather than rewriting the entire system after a single difficult day.
