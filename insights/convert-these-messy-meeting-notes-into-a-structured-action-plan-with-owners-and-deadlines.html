Copy-and-use prompt

Act as a chief-of-staff meeting editor. Convert the notes below into an execution-ready action plan.

Meeting notes: [PASTE]

Return exactly:
1. DECISIONS — confirmed decisions only.
2. ACTION REGISTER — table with Action, Owner, Deadline, Dependency, Status.
3. OPEN QUESTIONS — unresolved items requiring a decision.
4. RISKS/BLOCKERS — issues that could stop execution.
5. FOLLOW-UP MESSAGE — a concise recap suitable for the attendees.

Never invent an owner, deadline, decision, or commitment. Use UNASSIGNED or DATE NEEDED when the notes do not provide one.

Implementation checklist

  1. Every action has an owner field.
  2. Every action has a deadline field or DATE NEEDED.
  3. Decisions are separated from tasks.
  4. Open questions are not presented as settled decisions.
  5. Nothing material is invented from context.

Operating protocol

  1. Extract confirmed decisions without rewriting them as tasks.
  2. Extract each action using a concrete verb.
  3. Attach the named owner and deadline only when present in the notes.
  4. Mark missing ownership, dates, or dependencies explicitly.
  5. Create a short follow-up list for unresolved questions and circulate the action register.

A practical check is simple: another person should be able to read the page and know what to do first, what evidence counts as progress, and when the task is complete. When any of those answers are vague, rewrite the instruction as a concrete verb plus an output. Clear execution language is more valuable than adding another layer of motivation.

Choose evidence that can be inspected. A saved draft, sent message, completed checklist, scheduled meeting, or updated record is stronger than a feeling that progress occurred. Visible evidence reduces self-negotiation and makes the next step easier to select.

It also helps an assistant or collaborator continue the work without reconstructing the entire context from memory.

Sequence the work so that uncertainty is resolved before effort compounds. Confirm the target, gather only the information required for the next decision, complete the highest-leverage action, and then review the result. Avoid mixing planning, execution, and evaluation in the same moment.
