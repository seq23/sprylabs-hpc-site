Deep work: plan a focus block you can finish

Deep work is sustained attention on a demanding task with a defined outcome and limited avoidable interruption. A practical focus block specifies what you will produce, what materials you need and how you will resume after a disruption. It is a working arrangement to adapt to your responsibilities, rather than a universal schedule.

Quick answer

Name an output rather than a broad subject. Replace work on strategy with draft the options section for the upcoming decision meeting.

What this guide is best for: people who reserve time for focused work but spend much of that time deciding where to begin.

How do you put this into practice?

Use the following sequence as a starting plan, then adjust it to the work you are responsible for.

  1. Name an output rather than a broad subject. Replace work on strategy with draft the options section for the upcoming decision meeting.
  2. Open the required documents and identify missing information before the block. If a dependency is unavailable, choose a feasible portion of the work.
  3. Agree on interruption boundaries with the people affected. Keep a way for genuinely urgent responsibilities to reach you.
  4. Write the next action on a visible note before stepping away. Leave enough context that returning does not require reconstructing the whole problem.
  5. Close by saving the output and stating what remains. Use the actual result to size the next block instead of assuming every calendar reservation was productive.

How long should a focus block be?

Choose a duration that fits the task and your current responsibilities rather than copying a universal target. A block must leave room to start and close the work without ignoring care or service obligations. If you cannot sustain the planned interval, reduce its scope and record why before concluding that focused work is impossible for you.

What should happen when an interruption is unavoidable?

Preserve a restart point and handle the interruption according to its importance. Write the open question, the next action and the location of the relevant material. A legitimate urgent request may need to replace the planned work entirely. Make that tradeoff visible in the record so an interrupted block does not silently look like completed capacity.

How can teams protect focused time?

Agree on what counts as urgent, where requests should go and when a response is normally expected. A calendar label alone cannot resolve competing obligations. Teams can rotate coverage, batch nonurgent questions or schedule collaboration around concentration work, depending on their role. Check whether the arrangement helps both delivery and access before making it permanent.

What would this look like in practice?

An analyst needs to compare contract options. Before the block, they open the current terms, identify the decision criteria and create the comparison table. A service escalation interrupts the analysis. The restart note names the unverified renewal clause and its document location. When the analyst returns, that note makes the next action visible without assuming the analysis was already finished.

Frequently asked questions

Is silence required for deep work?

No particular environment works for every person or task. Choose an arrangement that supports the output and accommodates the people around you. If noise is the actual barrier, change the location or agree on a boundary where feasible. If the barrier is an unresolved decision, a quieter room alone will not solve it.

Should you track hours or completed work?

Track whichever observation helps the next planning decision, while recognizing their difference. Reserved hours show an allocation of capacity; a completed output shows what resulted. Reviewing them together can reveal scope or interruption problems. Avoid treating time at a desk as proof that a demanding task has progressed.

Can AI do the focused work for you?

An AI assistant may help outline, summarize or suggest alternatives, but its output needs appropriate review. Keep confidential material in approved systems and verify any factual claim before relying on it. Decide which part of the task requires your judgment, then use tools to support that responsibility rather than obscure it.
