Identity-based execution: practical guide

Identity-based execution is a planning approach that translates a chosen value into an observable action rather than a label about who you are.

Quick answer

Replace a label such as organized person with one behavior another person could observe.

What this guide is best for: readers who need to make a concrete decision about identity-based execution.

How do you put this into practice?

Use the following sequence as a starting plan, then adjust it to the work you are responsible for.

  1. Replace a label such as organized person with one behavior another person could observe.
  2. Judge whether the action served the responsibility rather than whether it proved your identity.
  3. Record the action you chose, its intended result and the next occasion on which you will check it. Keep the record in a place you can return to without rebuilding your setup.

How do you start identity-based execution?

Replace a label such as organized person with one behavior another person could observe. Use a current commitment rather than a fictional ideal week. Check the actual calendar and inputs before treating the proposed action as feasible.

What should change your plan?

Judge whether the action served the responsibility rather than whether it proved your identity. When the conditions change, record the new constraint and choose again. Tell anyone affected by a changed commitment rather than silently moving its deadline.

What mistake should you avoid?

Avoid treating a missed action as evidence that you are the wrong kind of person. If the method creates more work than it helps you resolve, simplify the record before adding another tool. The plan remains a proposal until you compare it with what actually happened.

What would this look like in practice?

A team lead who values reliability records a deadline risk and tells the affected colleague before the deadline passes. This is an illustrative scenario, not a customer result or evidence of a measured improvement.

Frequently asked questions

What should you write down first?

Replace a label such as organized person with one behavior another person could observe. Keep the first record short enough to use during a real workday. Include the specific deliverable or decision and where the supporting information can be found.

How should you review the result?

Judge whether the action served the responsibility rather than whether it proved your identity. Compare the result with the commitment you actually made. Identify one useful change for the next attempt rather than rewriting the entire system after a single difficult day.
