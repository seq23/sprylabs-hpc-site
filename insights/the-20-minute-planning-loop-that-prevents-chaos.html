Bounded planning review: practical guide

Bounded planning review is a review with a stopping point that produces a chosen action rather than an ever larger plan; twenty minutes is an illustrative time box.

Quick answer

Gather current deadlines and identify the one decision that is preventing a start.

What this guide is best for: readers who need to make a concrete decision about bounded planning review.

How do you put this into practice?

Use the following sequence as a starting plan, then adjust it to the work you are responsible for.

  1. Gather current deadlines and identify the one decision that is preventing a start.
  2. End the review when you have a feasible next action and a place to do it.
  3. Record the action you chose, its intended result and the next occasion on which you will check it. Keep the record in a place you can return to without rebuilding your setup.

What would this look like in practice?

A freelancer uses a short review to choose which revision is due first, then closes the task list and opens that document. This is an illustrative scenario, not a customer result or evidence of a measured improvement.
