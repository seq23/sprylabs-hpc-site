Bounded planning review: practical guide

Bounded planning review is a review with a stopping point that produces a chosen action rather than an ever larger plan; twenty minutes is an illustrative time box.

Quick answer

Gather current deadlines and identify the one decision that is preventing a start.

What this guide is best for: readers who need to make a concrete decision about bounded planning review.

How do you put this into practice?

Use the following sequence as a starting plan, then adjust it to the work you are responsible for.

  1. Gather current deadlines and identify the one decision that is preventing a start.
  2. End the review when you have a feasible next action and a place to do it.
  3. Record the action you chose, its intended result and the next occasion on which you will check it. Keep the record in a place you can return to without rebuilding your setup.

How do you start bounded planning review?

Gather current deadlines and identify the one decision that is preventing a start. Use a current commitment rather than a fictional ideal week. Check the actual calendar and inputs before treating the proposed action as feasible.

What should change your plan?

End the review when you have a feasible next action and a place to do it. When the conditions change, record the new constraint and choose again. Tell anyone affected by a changed commitment rather than silently moving its deadline.

What mistake should you avoid?

Do not spend the entire available work period refining the planning system. If the method creates more work than it helps you resolve, simplify the record before adding another tool. The plan remains a proposal until you compare it with what actually happened.

What would this look like in practice?

A freelancer uses a short review to choose which revision is due first, then closes the task list and opens that document. This is an illustrative scenario, not a customer result or evidence of a measured improvement.

Frequently asked questions

What should you write down first?

Gather current deadlines and identify the one decision that is preventing a start. Keep the first record short enough to use during a real workday. Include the specific deliverable or decision and where the supporting information can be found.

How should you review the result?

End the review when you have a feasible next action and a place to do it. Compare the result with the commitment you actually made. Identify one useful change for the next attempt rather than rewriting the entire system after a single difficult day.
