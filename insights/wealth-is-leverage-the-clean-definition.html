Leverage in everyday work: practical guide

Leverage in everyday work is the reuse of an asset or process across useful tasks; it is not a promise of financial gain.

Quick answer

Find a recurring task whose inputs and acceptable output are already understood.

What this guide is best for: readers who need to make a concrete decision about leverage in everyday work.

How do you put this into practice?

Use the following sequence as a starting plan, then adjust it to the work you are responsible for.

  1. Find a recurring task whose inputs and acceptable output are already understood.
  2. Reuse a template when the task repeats; review it when the task’s requirements change.
  3. Record the action you chose, its intended result and the next occasion on which you will check it. Keep the record in a place you can return to without rebuilding your setup.

What would this look like in practice?

A service business reuses a project handoff checklist but checks the actual recipient, dates and attachments on every handoff. This is an illustrative scenario, not a customer result or evidence of a measured improvement.
